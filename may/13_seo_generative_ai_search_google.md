# SEO Is Not Dead — It Just Got a New Boss

Google's official guidance on optimizing for generative AI search, distilled into what actually matters.

---

- **SEO and AI search are the same thing.** Google's generative AI features are built on top of the same core ranking and quality systems that power traditional Search. "AEO" (Answer Engine Optimization) and "GEO" (Generative Engine Optimization) are just SEO with a new label. There is no separate optimization track.

- **RAG is how Google AI answers questions.** When you ask Google's AI a question, it runs Retrieval-Augmented Generation — pulling relevant, up-to-date pages from its Search index and grounding the response in that content. Your page still needs to be crawled, indexed, and ranked to be cited.

- **Query fan-out means your page can be found for queries you never wrote.** Google generates concurrent related sub-queries from a user's original question. "How to fix a lawn full of weeds" spawns "best herbicides for lawns", "remove weeds without chemicals", and more. Your page doesn't need exact keyword matches — the AI handles semantic bridging.

- **Unique, non-commodity content is the highest-leverage investment.** AI systems aggregate sources and surface the ones with a distinct point of view. A first-hand review beats a summary of existing content. "Why We Waived the Inspection & Saved Money" beats "7 Tips for First-Time Homebuyers." Your personal experience and expertise are the moat that AI-generated content cannot replicate.

- **Write for humans, not for AI parsers.** Clear paragraph structure, good headings, readable prose — these help both your readers and Google's systems understand your content. There is no special writing style required for AI search. AI understands synonyms and intent, so chasing long-tail keyword variations is not a strategy.

- **Technical SEO fundamentals still gate everything.** A page must be indexed and eligible to appear with a snippet before it can be cited in any generative AI response. Crawlability, page experience, latency, mobile rendering, JavaScript SEO — all of it still applies. Nothing in AI search bypasses these requirements.

- **`llms.txt` files and AI-specific markup do nothing for Google.** Google may crawl many file types, but no special machine-readable file, Markdown file, or custom markup schema gives you preferential treatment in generative AI responses. This is one of the most circulated myths and one of the least supported by how Google actually works.

- **Do not "chunk" your content for AI.** Google's systems can understand multiple topics on a single page and surface the relevant piece to users. There is no ideal page length and no requirement to break content into fragments. Write for your audience's reading experience, not for an imagined AI parser.

- **Inauthentic "mentions" are filtered, not rewarded.** Generative AI features surface what is being said about products and services across the web, but the same spam systems that govern traditional Search apply here. Engineering fake mentions across blogs and forums is treated as spam, not signal.

- **Structured data helps but is not required for AI search.** No special schema.org markup unlocks generative AI visibility. Continue using structured data for rich results eligibility in traditional Search — that benefit carries forward — but do not add it specifically as an AI optimization tactic.

- **Local and ecommerce details benefit from dedicated Google products.** For product listings and local business information, Merchant Center feeds and Google Business Profiles are the direct path to appearing in AI-generated responses. These are separate from general SEO and worth maintaining independently.

- **Browser agents are the next frontier worth watching.** AI agents that autonomously book reservations, compare products, and complete tasks on behalf of users access websites by analyzing visual renderings, DOM structure, and accessibility trees. The emerging Universal Commerce Protocol (UCP) is early but directional — websites that are agent-friendly will have a structural advantage as agentic experiences expand inside Google Search.

---

**The one-line summary:** Stop optimizing for AI search. Keep optimizing for your readers. Google's AI will find the rest.
