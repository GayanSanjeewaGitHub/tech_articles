# The Questions Every Developer Should Ask: Reflections from the AI Coding Summit

## What Are You Really Working On?

In 1986, mathematician Richard Hamming asked his colleagues at Bell Labs a devastating pair of questions: "What's the most important problem in your field?" And then, immediately after: "So why aren't you working on that?"

**Now, turn that mirror on yourself.** What's the most important problem in technology today? If you paused, considered it, and answered "AI" — then here's the uncomfortable follow-up: Are you working on it? Or are you merely watching from the sidelines?

## What If You're Building Yesterday's Future?

Imagine you're constructing a building while the foundation of architecture itself is being rewritten. Every line of code you write today might be written differently tomorrow — or not written by you at all. 

**What if the patterns you're mastering right now become obsolete before you perfect them?** This isn't hypothetical fear-mongering. AI coding agents are already here. Google's Jules, GitHub Copilot, Cursor AI — they're not coming; they've arrived.

So the question isn't whether AI will transform how we build software. The question is: **Will you be shaping that transformation, or will it shape you?**

## Why Does This Matter to Your Brain?

Here's where neuroscience meets career strategy. Your brain learns best when confronted with meaningful problems that create cognitive dissonance. Reading about AI coding is passive. But asking yourself, "How would I architect this differently if an AI was my pair programmer?" — that activates deeper neural pathways.

**Imagine this scenario:** Tomorrow, your team gets access to an AI agent that can write 80% of your boilerplate code. What happens to your role? Do you become obsolete, or do you evolve into something more valuable?

The uncomfortable truth: **You only become obsolete if you keep doing what the AI can do.** But if you shift to the problems AI can't solve — system design, business logic, cross-team collaboration, ethical considerations — you become irreplaceable.

## What Are the Real Stakes?

The 2025 AI Engineering Summit wasn't just another tech conference. It represented an inflection point. When industry leaders gather specifically around "AI coding patterns, systems, and products," they're not discussing a feature — they're discussing the future of the entire software industry.

**What if this moment is like the shift from assembly to high-level languages?** Developers who clung to assembly language didn't become more valuable; they became anachronistic. Those who embraced C, Java, and Python shaped the next 40 years of computing.

## The Question That Changes Everything

Here's the meta-question, the one that ties everything together:

**If you knew that in five years, AI would handle 90% of routine coding, what would you start learning today?**

Would you double down on syntax and frameworks? Or would you pivot toward prompt engineering, AI system design, architecture, product thinking, and the uniquely human skills of creativity and judgment?

The summit's core message wasn't about tools or technologies. It was about agency. The developers who thrive won't be those who resist AI coding or those who blindly depend on it. They'll be the ones who ask better questions, solve harder problems, and use AI as a lever to amplify their unique human capabilities.

**So, one final question: What's your next move?**
